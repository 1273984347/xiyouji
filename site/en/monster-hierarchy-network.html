I · The Four-Tier Structure of Monster Hierarchy

FOUR-TIER HIERARCHY OF MONSTER ORGANIZATION
Definition: The monsters of the Journey to the West are no disorganized rabble; rather, they form a four-tier hierarchy ordered by "sphere of influence + blood/contract ties + distance from Heaven." Lone Wolf — fights alone, with no home or reinforcements, and is the easiest to destroy (White Bone Demon, Spider Demon, Nine-Headed Demon); Family Clan — bound by blood, defends across strongholds, and is most concerned with inheritance (the Bull Demon King family, the Gold Horn and Silver Horn brothers); Alliance — sworn allies of different names, ranked by strength, sharing territory and intelligence (the three demons of Lion-Camel Ridge, the three demons of Chechi); Celestial Bureaucrat — holds an official Heavenly post, so descending to earth is a "business trip" or "desertion of duty," and their fate is a return to office rather than destruction (Yellow Robe Demon is Kui Wood Wolf; Yellow Wind Demon is Lingji’s mount). The four-tier structure reveals the core law of "monster politics" in the Journey to the West: the closer one stands to Heaven, the lower the chance of destruction and the higher the chance of recruitment.

II · Monster Hierarchy Force-Directed Graph

FORCE-DIRECTED GRAPH · NODE SIZE = POWER · COLOR = TYPE
Definition: With 17 core monsters as nodes, node size maps "sphere of influence (power 1-10)" and color maps "organizational form." Edges fall into four types: Subordination (descended under Heaven’s roster), Alliance (sworn allies of different names), Hostility (direct conflict with Wukong’s camp), and Blood (parent/child, husband/wife, or sibling ties among family-clan monsters). The force-directed graph directly renders four topologies: "family clans band together, alliances form triangles, lone wolves stand isolated, and bureaucrats lean on Heaven."

III · Monster–Heaven Sankey Diagram

SANKEY · ORIGIN → FATE
Definition: The Sankey diagram shows the "origin → fate" flow of monsters. Origin falls into five categories: Wild, Mount, Attendant, Constellation, and Other; Fate falls into four: Recruited (absorbed into the Heaven/Buddhist system), Destroyed (killed or dissolved), Escaped (never subdued), and Returned (official monsters returning to their original post). The flows reveal the core political law of the Journey to the West: the closer an origin is to Heaven (Constellation / Attendant / Mount), the more the fate tends toward "Returned" — the ones actually destroyed on the westward road are almost all "wild monsters."

IV · Monster Ability Radar Chart

RADAR · 5 DIMENSIONS · 14 MONSTERS
Definition: Five dimensions assess a monster’s overall strength — Combat (frontal fighting power), Magic (supernatural transformations), Cunning (schemes and stratagems), Influence (territory and minions), and Outcome (a final-fate score, where Returned > Recruited > Escaped > Destroyed). The larger the radar area, the higher the monster’s overall rank. The Golden-Winged Roc and Bull Demon King have the largest areas; White Bone Demon has the smallest area but stands out on Cunning — revealing the two survival strategies: "the lone wolf survives on cunning, the bureaucrat survives on their post."

V · Monster Fate Timeline

FATE TIMELINE · BY CHAPTER ORDER
Definition: Twenty monster fate nodes are arranged in chapter order and shown on four separate tracks by organizational form. Each node marks a key event (appearance / alliance / subjugation / fate), with color distinguishing the stage: Rise (the rise period), Conflict (frontal conflict with the scripture-seeking party), and Fate (the final outcome). The timeline renders four rhythms: "lone wolves die out fast, family clans tangle across chapters, alliances erupt all at once, and bureaucrats return to their posts."

Origin → Fate Distribution (donut chart)

  • Rank is strongly tied to origin: Of the 17 monsters, 9 are wild (53%), and 6 of those are destroyed; all 8 mounts / attendants / constellations are returned or recruited. On the westward road, "those who should die almost all die, and those who should not almost never do" — behind the hierarchy lies Heaven’s prejudgment and allotment of lives.
  • Family clans are the hardest to handle: The Bull Demon King family spans a 22-chapter tangle from Chapter 40 to 61, the longest-running opponent on the westward road. The "difficulty" of family-clan monsters lies not in any single combatant but in cross-generation defense, cross-stronghold rescue, and cross-role resource mobilization.
  • Alliances dissolve through the system: The combined might of the three Lion-Camel Ridge demons far exceeds Wukong, and in the end they are only broken up when the Tathāgata, Manjushri, and Samantabhadra appear together. The three Chechi demons, by contrast, die in the "contest of magic" — what alliance monsters fear most is not force but the legitimacy challenge of being defeated one by one.
