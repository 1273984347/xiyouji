I · Intertextuality Network Overview

OVERVIEW: INTERTEXTUALITY FORCE-DIRECTED GRAPH
Definition: Intertextuality was proposed by Kristeva in 1966; it holds that any text is an absorption and transformation of other texts. This graph centers on Journey to the West and radiates outward to seven precursor texts (the Scripture-Fetching Tale, Great Tang Records on the Western Regions, Biography of the Tripitaka Master, Paktongsa Ŏnhae, Journey to the West Zaju, the Buddhist Canon, and the Daoist Canon), connecting four intertextuality theorists (Kristeva, Barthes, Genette, Riffaterre) into a "precursor text – target text – theorist" tripartite network. Node colors distinguish type, and each edge is labeled with one of Genette's five types.

Node color: by text type · node size = degree · edge color = intertextual type · draggable

II · Genette's Five Types × Precursor Texts Matrix

MATRIX: GENETTE'S FIVE TYPES × PRECURSOR TEXTS
Definition: In Palimpsestes (1982), Genette divides transtextual relations into five types: Citation, Allusion, Commentary, Transformation, and Translation. The matrix chart uses the seven precursor texts as rows and the five types as columns, with color intensity representing intertextual strength (0–3). The Scripture-Fetching Tale is strongest on "Transformation" (the shaping of the Sun Wukong figure), while the Buddhist and Daoist canons are strongest on "Allusion" (the permeation of ideas and themes).

III · Precursor Text Intertextual-Strength Radar

RADAR: FIVE INTERTEXTUAL DIMENSIONS PER TEXT
Definition: Each precursor text's intertextual strength relative to Journey to the West is assessed on five dimensions: Structure (narrative framework), Theme (core ideas), Character (characterization), Plot (story events), and Language (word and phrase allusions). The larger the radar area, the more strongly the text shaped Journey to the West. The Scripture-Fetching Tale and the Journey to the West Zaju are strongest on "Character" and "Plot", while Paktongsa Ŏnhae stands out on "Language".

IV · Precursor Text → Journey to the West Intertextual Timeline

TIMELINE: PRECURSOR TEXTS THROUGH THE AGES
Definition: The eight texts (including Journey to the West itself) are arranged chronologically, with each text's completion era and main intertextual type marked. The span runs from the 6th century BCE (Dao De Jing) to the 17th century (Paktongsa Ŏnhae), covering more than 2,200 years. Color distinguishes text type, and node size reflects intertextual strength, revealing the textual genealogy and intertextual accumulation that preceded the compilation of Journey to the West.

V · Four Theorists Intertextuality Sankey Diagram

SANKEY: FOUR THEORISTS × THEORY DIMENSIONS × APPLICATIONS
Definition: The Sankey diagram shows a three-layer flow: four theorists → theory dimensions → Journey to the West applications. Kristeva's "intertextuality" maps to the Journey to the West precursor-text network; Barthes's "death of the author" maps to the Wu Cheng'en authorship question; Genette's "five types" map to the citation and transformation in Journey to the West; and Riffaterre's "hypertextuality" maps to Journey to the West as a hypertext. Flow thickness corresponds to each theory's application weight in Journey to the West studies.
