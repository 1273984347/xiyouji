An Imagery Lineage — Stone, Peach, Bone, Sutra

ESSAY E33 · SUMMARY TRANSLATION
Four images (stone-monkey, peach, white-bone, true-sutra) trace Creation→Desire→Illusion→Awakening, read through Pound, Eliot, Bachelard, Liu Xie.
A summary translation of 西游诗词意象谱系专题.md. The Chinese original reads the novel's verse through three poet theorists and six frame couplets (E31), three classes of scene-poem (E32), and four imagery theorists reading a four-image lineage (E33). All line numbers and chapter titles are carried over from the source without fabrication.

Four Theorists of Imagery ↔ Pound · Eliot · Bachelard · Liu

An image differs from a scene: a scene is "objective view," an image is "the image within meaning" (Liu Xie: "gaze on the image, then wield the axe"). The book's four most lineage-bearing images are the Stone Monkey (creation), the Peach (desire), the White Bone (illusion), and the True Sutra (awakening) — a complete lineage of creation → desire → illusion → awakening.

Creation: the Stone Monkey ↔ ch. 1, line 522

"Since the world's opening it received heaven's truth and earth's grace, the sun's essence and moon's bloom; long steeped, it gained spiritual awareness. Within bred an immortal embryo; one day it split, bore a stone egg like a ball, and met the wind, became a stone monkey." The stone-monkey is the lineage's creation origin — heaven's unmade, desire-less image. Pound's "direct treatment": Wu writes the thing itself with three verbs (split, bear, transform), no abstract sermon. Bachelard's earth-image: the immortal stone bears creation. Liu Xie's "spirit roams with things": Wu's spirit roams the immortal-stone object and generates the image.

Desire: the Peach ↔ ch. 5, line 726

Wukong raids the Peach Garden, eating peaches of three-thousand, six-thousand, nine-thousand years' ripening, then the Jade Pool banquet, then Laozi's golden elixir. The peach is the lineage's desire turn — the image of immortal longevity's desire. Eliot's objective correlative: the peach is desire's formula; when it appears, desiring feeling is instantly aroused (three ripenings = small/middle/great desire). Bachelard's water-image: peach-juice flows, water bears desire's fluidity. Liu Xie's "gaze on the image, wield the axe": Wu glimpses the peach-desire image and presents it as a feast. From the stone's desire-less to the peach's desire-full — the lineage's turn.

Illusion: the White Bone ↔ ch. 27, line 2306

The Corpse-Demon thrice tempts the Tang Monk, transformed as a maiden, an old woman, an old man; Wukong thrice strikes her back to "a pile of powdery skulls." The white bone is the lineage's illusion deepening — the image of "form is empty." Pound's "complex of intellect and emotion": the skull instantaneously unites intellect (form-is-empty) and feeling (fear, pity). Bachelard's air-image: bone dissolves to smoke, air bears illusion's dissipation. Liu Xie's "spirit penetrates through image": Wu passes the bone-illusion image through the truth of emptiness. The three transformations (maiden / mother / elder) are illusion's three disguises — desire dissolved by illusion.

Awakening: the True Sutra ↔ ch. 100, line 7085

The Tang Monk returns with thirty-five divisions, 5,048 scrolls; the Five Sages become perfected. The sutra is the lineage's awakening round-completion — the image of enlightened mind-nature. Eliot's objective correlative rounded: the sutra is awakening's formula (thirty-five divisions / 5,048 scrolls = small/middle/great awakening). Bachelard's fire-image: the sutra illuminates the mind, fire bears awakening's light. Liu Xie's image-generation rounded: from spirit roaming the sutra-awakening object, to wielding the axe, to spirit penetrating — the path completes. The lineage from creation → desire → illusion → awakening closes in the sutra's awakening.

East Meets West ↔ Liu Xie, 1,400 years ahead

Liu Xie (China, c. 500) precedes Pound (1913), Eliot (1919), and Bachelard (1957) by over 1,400 years yet already proposed a complete image-generation theory — the earliness of Chinese imagery theory is visible here. Wu Cheng'en (c. 1500–1582) inherited Liu Xie and unfolded the four-image lineage four centuries before Western Imagism. The contemporary meaning: the imagery lineage is not only a classical structure but a modern life-path of creation → desire → illusion → awakening.
