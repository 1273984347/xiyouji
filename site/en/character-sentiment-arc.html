VI · Key Insights

KEY INSIGHTS
Definition:Eight key insights into the character sentiment arcs distilled from the four-layer NLP data W100+W102+W103+W121, covering the five pilgrims’ affective traits, relationship-pair tension, U-curve evidence, turning-point analysis, and data limitations.
  1. Sun Wukong swings the most: as the narrative engine (53.6% of quotations), his sentiment curve has the widest amplitude; Ch.27 (banished after the White Bone Demon) and Ch.57 (the Six-Eared Macaque) are the two negative troughs, while Ch.14 (taking vows) and Ch.98 (the golden hoop falls away) are the two positive peaks.
  2. Tripitaka is the steadiest but skewed negative: as the vehicle of the superego, his curve fluctuates little yet averages negative (−0.18), reflecting the "mission-driven one"persistent tension of the “mission-driven one”. At Ch.27, when he expels Wukong, a rare intense negative spike appears.
  3. Zhu Bajie stays consistently negative: avg_sentiment −0.41 is the lowest of the five; negative-affect words appear frequently (split up · pack luggage · famished), confirming his "id–desire mediator"role as the “id–desire mediator”.
  4. Sha Wujing is closest to neutral: avg_sentiment +0.12 is the most positive of the five, yet he has only 318 quotations (4.8%), "silent majority"so the “silent majority’s” affective portrait is sparse.
  5. The Tripitaka–Sun Wukong main axis: co-occurrence weight 88 is the highest in the book, sentiment mean −0.32 (negative), confirming a "high-intensity + high-conflict"“high-intensity + high-conflict” master-disciple tension that is the dual core of the whole relationship network.
  6. Evidence for the three U-curve phases: bucketed sentiment means for the decline (Ch.1–27) · trough (Ch.27–57) · rise (Ch.57–100) phases are −0.18 / −0.35 / −0.08, a clear U shape.
  7. Six key turning points: Ch.14 vows · Ch.27 expulsion · Ch.31 return · Ch.57 the two minds · Ch.81 perceiving the demon · Ch.98 the hoop falls away — forming Wukong’s "rebel → return"“rebel → return” pattern of affective evidence.
  8. Data limitations: the zero-dependency sentiment lexicon cannot recognise irony or metaphor; Bajie’s "split up"“split up”, though a negative word, functions as comic relief; White Dragon Horse cannot enter the sentiment-profile analysis with 0 quotations.
