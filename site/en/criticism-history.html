Lu Xun's Scalpel

Lu Xun uses a scalpel to cut open the text of Journey to the West, stripping away the Qing-era accretions of the "Golden Elixir Way" and exposing the dynamic emergence of its core vocabulary — repositioning it as a novel of gods and demons. Yet demystification itself is also a kind of re-enchantment: swapping "Golden Elixir" for "human feelings" is still another reading.
Lu Xun, A Brief History of Chinese Fiction (1923): "The author was by nature fond of humor and jest, so that even when recounting bizarre and illusory events he would mix in words that bring a smile, making gods and demons all have human feelings, and spirits too versed in the ways of the world…" — With the four words "a work of play," he set the tone, rescuing Journey to the West from the accretions of the "Golden Elixir Way" and placing it among novels of gods and demons. This cut opened up the cipher book of the Qing Inner Alchemy school, yet it also inaugurated the hegemony of another reading.
