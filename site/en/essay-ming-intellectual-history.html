The Pilgrimage as Intellectual History ↔ the thesis

The scripture pilgrimage is not mere mythic adventure; it is the literary replication of Ming intellectual history. "Mind-ape" (xinyuan) and "intention-horse" (yima) run through the book — the mythologised form of Wang Yangming's "heart-mind learning" (xinxue). Red Boy styles himself the "Sage-Infant King" — a mirror of Li Zhi's "childlike mind." Zhu Bajie's earthy "food and sex are nature" is perfectly isomorphic with the Taizhou School's "the everyday is the Way." The Jade Emperor ruling Heaven and the Buddha presiding over the West form a "Heaven-West dual track" — the mythologised projection of Huang Zongxi's "the people are host, the ruler is guest." Written amid the turbulence of mid-late Ming thought (Wang Yangming's rise against Cheng-Zhu orthodoxy, the Taizhou "wild Chan," Li Zhi martyred as a heretic, Huang Zongxi's early enlightenment), the novel casts all of it onto the pilgrimage.

Distinction from E15. Essay E15 reads the novel through the "Three Teachings as One" framework (Confucian governance, Daoist self-cultivation, Buddhist mind) — a synthesis of traditions. This essay instead reads it through four individual Ming thinkers as case studies, completing the eighth layer of the project's "eight-layer Ming mirror" (politics + economy + deities + judiciary + military + exam + religion + thought). Religion is outer discipline; thought is inner awakening.

Four Thinkers ↔ the scaffold

  • Wang Yangming (1472–1529): "the mind is principle," "extending innate knowing" (zhiliangzhi), "the unity of knowing and acting." His "crush the thieves in the heart" (po zhongxinzei) is exactly Wukong slaying the six thieves. The "Four-Sentence Teaching" maps onto Wukong's four cultivation stages.
  • Li Zhi (1527–1602): the "childlike mind" (tongxin) — the true, undefiled original mind — and self-styled "heterodoxy" (yiduan). Martyred in prison (1602). Red Boy's "Sage-Infant" is the mythologised childlike mind; his defiance is the mythologised heretic.
  • Wang Ji (1498–1583), Taizhou School: the "Four-Nothings" (siwu) and "the everyday is the Way." Bajie's "food and sex" is the mythologised everyday-Way; the Taizhou "every street a sage" mirrors demons who still attain Buddhahood.
  • Huang Zongxi (1610–1695), Waiting for the Dawn: "the people are host, the ruler is guest" (junkezhulilun) and "schools deliberate affairs." The Heaven-West dual track is its mythologised projection — a century before Rousseau.

The Eight-Layer Ming Mirror ↔ the closure

With this essay the project closes its "eight-layer Ming mirror": W126 politics (bureaucracy = Heaven's structure), W130 economy (salary = Peach ration), W134 deities (deities = state bureaucracy), W142 judiciary (Heavenly Law = the Code), W146 military (heavenly troops = weisuo garrison), W150 exam (merit = Buddhahood), W154 religion (ordination certificate = pilgrimage qualification), and W180 thought (heart-mind = mind-ape, childlike mind = sage-infant, everyday-Way = food-sex, people-host-ruler-guest = Heaven-West dual track). The first seven expose the "hard structure" of state machinery and its "soft mechanisms"; thought (W180) exposes the "inner awakening" — strong structure, smooth mechanism, fixed soul-order, yet without awakening the empire is but a shell.
