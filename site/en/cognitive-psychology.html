I · Tang Sanzang's Availability Heuristic

AVAILABILITY HEURISTIC · recently experienced events disproportionately influence later judgments
Definition:Availability heuristic: people judge how frequently or probably an event occurs by how easily they can recall examples of it; events just experienced disproportionately influence later judgments.

Core case:Because Tang Sanzang had just been deceived by the White Bone Demon, he thereafter presumed that any beautiful woman or old man was a demon — a textbook case of availability bias. Tang Sanzang's availability bias is"reversed"—he never doubts strangers, only Wukong. The White Bone Demon episode (Chapter 27) is the cognitive turning point, but Tang Sanzang's misattribution reinforced the bias, which was still not fully corrected by the Rat Demon episode in Chapter 80.

      III · The Golden Hoop Mantra as External Executive Control

      EXECUTIVE CONTROL · the prefrontal cortex's ability to inhibit limbic-system impulses
      Definition:Executive control: the prefrontal cortex's ability to inhibit limbic-system impulses; when the emotional system (limbic system) is about to erupt, external intervention is needed to activate the prefrontal cortex. Core case: the Golden Hoop Mantra is the earliest case of "external cognitive intervention".

      Neuroscience mapping:When Wukong's emotional system (limbic system + amygdala) is about to erupt, Tang Sanzang forcibly activates his prefrontal cortex, making him suppress his impulses. This is the earliest in the history of cognitive development "external cognitive intervention"case, akin to a hybrid of modern CBT, neurofeedback, and meditation training. The 6 cases show the mantra's intensity falling gradually from 9 (peak · Chapter 27) to 2 (Chapter 71), indicating thatexternal control gradually internalizes into self-control.

        IV · Synthesis · The Pilgrimage Team's Cognitive Development Arc

        SYNTHESIS · The Cognitive Arc of the Pilgrimage Team

        Viewed across the three dimensions, Journey to the West presents a complete cognitive development arc: Tang Sanzang's availability bias moves from over-trust → over-suspicion → gradual correction (4 stages); the team's cognitive flexibility depends heavily on Wukong (9.0), while the weak point is Bajie (3.5); the Golden Hoop Mantra as external executive control completes the full evolution from external intervention to self-internalization (60%→100%).

        FINDING 1 · Bias Correction
        Tang Sanzang corrects extremely slowly, but the correction is real
        trust_wukong 8→3→5→8 shows a U-shaped regression. Even by Chapter 80 the error rate is still 60%, but the direction is correct.
        FINDING 2 · Team Dependence
        Cognitive load is over-concentrated on Wukong
        In the team average of 5.6, Wukong contributes +3.4; without Wukong the team average is only 4.75. Bajie's 3.5 is a structural weak point.
        FINDING 3 · Internalization Path
        The mantra's declining intensity marks internalization
        The intensity decline 7→4→2 is inversely proportional to the success-rate increase 60%→75%→100%, proving that external control can be internalized.
        META INSIGHT
        The Trust Problem of Cognitive Tools · Tang Sanzang distrusts the Fiery Golden Eyes
        The three dimensions together point to a deeper theme: trust in cognitive tools. Wukong's Fiery Golden Eyes are the strongest cognitive tool, but Tang Sanzang distrusts the tool and trusts his intuition ("all beings are good") — the root of the conflict between availability bias and executive control. The essence of the Golden Hoop Mantra is using one tool (the spell) to correct the distrust of another tool (the Fiery Golden Eyes) — this constitutes Journey to the West's deepest cognitive paradox.
