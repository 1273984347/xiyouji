Readers Write Back to the Journey ↔ project creations

Beyond scholarly exegesis, the project keeps a “themed creation” column where contributors write Journey-inspired verse in any form — five-character quatrain, seven-character quatrain, a ci lyric, antique-style, or free verse — under the motto “old bottle, new wine.” Submissions carry a form, a theme, and the chapter they answer. What follows are four representative pieces (authored by the project, 2026-07), presented here in faithful translation. They are modern creative readings, not part of the sixteenth-century novel — evidence of the text's living reception.

Five-Element Mountain ↔ 5-char quatrain · ch. 7–14

The mountain presses five hundred years;
wind and snow cross between the brows.
This heart is no wood, no stone —
still it gazes toward the blue sky.

Theme: the unrepressed heart. Beneath the mountain, the heart that cannot be crushed keeps watching the sky.

Three Strikes on White Bone ↔ 7-char quatrain · ch. 27

Fire-eyes thrice see through the transformed shape;
one chant of the circlet — the pain is clear.
To slay the demon, the master need not comprehend;
alone he turns back to Flower-Fruit, the hills all green.

Theme: seeing clearly and being driven out. The quatrain compresses the whole tragedy of ch. 27 into four lines.

True and False Monkey King ↔ Línjiāngxiān (ci) · ch. 57–58

One staff astounds heaven; clouds and waters in turmoil —
the Six-Ears is of the same root.
Beneath Spirit Mountain's seat, true and false are told apart:
the face before you was originally me —
smash the body within the mirror.

The bowl falls — one sound, heaven and earth grow still;
all dust of this realm is extinguished.
The two minds were one mind divided;
true and false, ask no more —
both are given to the traces within the bowl.

Theme: the two minds (èrxīn). The ci reads the false-monkey crisis as a splitting of the self.

Lingyun Ford ↔ 7-char regulated verse · ch. 98

At Lingyun Ford, water and sky stretch long;
the Guide Buddha poles a bottomless boat.
The fleshly body sheds away, following the flowing water;
the mortal bones are lightly cast into the vastness.
The five companions together return to Vulture Peak;
one heart bathes in the purple-gold light.
The hundred-and-eight-thousand-li scripture road —
given to a wisp of incense on the river's flow.

Theme: casting off the body. The regulated verse marks the pilgrims' passage from flesh to liberation.

Old Bottle, New Wine ↔ a living tradition

The column's premise — “old bottle, new wine; the wine too will grow old, yet someone always brews” — treats the novel as an open form. These four pieces show readers answering the book in its own metres, proof that the Journey keeps generating new verse centuries after Wu Cheng'en.
