The Lost Pinghua Layer

ESSAY · SUMMARY TRANSLATION
Between the Song shihua and the 1592 Shide tang edition lies a folded gap of four hundred years.
Source: A summary translation of docs/04-文化与历史背景/版本演变补遗-平话层.md. The original Chinese essay supplements the version-history overview by excavating the佚失 (lost) oral-prose pinghua stratum between the Southern-Song shihua and the Ming complete edition. This English version preserves the argument in condensed form; reconstructions are explicitly marked as conjecture.

The Problem: A Folded Four Hundred Years ↔ gap in the chain

The earliest complete hundred-chapter edition is the 1592 Shide tang print, yet the story's embryo already existed in the Southern-Song Datang Sanzang qujing shihua — there the Monkey Pilgrim is a gentle "White-Robed Scholar," and Sanzang is still the narrative protagonist. Between them lie roughly four hundred years of Yuan and early-Ming text history. What we can "establish" from this span amounts to a single relatively complete surviving drama — Yang Jingxian's Journey to the West zaju. The rest — storytellers' prompt-books, workshop pinghua, abridged manuscripts — mostly never entered woodblock circulation and vanished in war and worm-rot. The "lost pinghua layer" refers to this intermediate zone that textual history repeatedly acknowledges as "having existed" yet rarely preserves in full. It supplied flesh to the Shide tang edition without leaving its own name under the carver's knife.

· · ·

The Zaju Layer: Yang Jingxian and the Fixing of "Great Sage" ↔定型 stage

Yang Jingxian's (or Yang Na's) six-book, twenty-four-act Journey to the West zaju is the most reliable surviving specimen above the pinghua layer and the pivot of dramatized fixation. Its contributions to image-history are threefold. First, the migration of titles. The shihua's Monkey is a mild White-Robed Scholar; by the zaju, Sun the Pilgrim calls himself "Great Sage Who Reaches Heaven," with "Equal-to-Heaven" perhaps his brother's name — the pugnacious aura already appears. By Shide tang it consolidates into Wukong's self-proclaimed, then heaven-acknowledged, "Equal-to-Heaven Great Sage," completing the turn from "helping scholar" to "rebel demon-king." Second, the prototype of "Havoc in Heaven" takes shape — stealing peaches, stealing immortal robes, fighting heaven's troops; Flower-Fruit Mountain and Water-Curtain Cave are roughly fixed here. Third, the team expands dramaturgically: Zhu Bajie and Sha Seng enter, the "three disciples protect the master" pattern first forms — direct evidence that the pinghua layer expanded the shihua's single-thread tale into multi-character narrative. Drama, as the "fixing layer," framed fluid oral story into a repeatable stage structure for the first time.

· · ·

The Pinghua Layer: From Yongle Canon to Poktongsa ↔ conjectural

Above the zaju lies a Yuan-dynasty Journey to the West pinghua now wholly lost. Evidence that it "existed" comes from two fragmentary sources. One is a surviving Yongle Canon scroll (vol. 13139, "song" rhyme) which records the episode "Dream: Beheading the Jing River Dragon" and explicitly notes it is quoted from the Journey to the West pinghua — enough to establish that a written Yuan pinghua existed and already contained the "Wei Zheng beheads the dragon" thread, ancestor of chapters 9–10 of Shide tang. The other is the Korean-Lee-dynasty Chinese textbook Poktongsa Ŏnhae, whose dialogue and glosses cite the pinghua's plot outline — mentioning Wei Zheng beheading the dragon, Tang Taizong touring the underworld, Tripitaka's origin, the Chechi Kingdom contest. These are the only surviving "glints" of the pinghua layer. From them we may conjecture (not establish) that the Yuan pinghua already possessed a fairly complete front structure of "Wei Zheng beheads dragon — Tang king enters underworld — pilgrimage origin" and demon-subduing episodes like the Chechi contest, broadly inherited by the later hundred-chapter order. The full pinghua text is gone; its length, chapter count, and wording are unknowable. All such links are reasonable inference from fragmentary quotation; the real Yuan pinghua has sunk into the "lost layer" itself.

· · ·

The Abridged Layer: The Ancestor-Text Dispute ↔ preserved remnant

The Ming also produced "abridged editions" — Tang Sanzang xiyou shie zhuan (attrib. Zhu Dingchen) and Xiyou ji zhuan (attrib. Yang Zhihe) — far shorter than the hundred-chapter book. Their relation to Shide tang is a longstanding puzzle. One view (Lu Xun, Hu Shi) holds the abridged are condensations of the fuller Shide tang line, produced later by booksellers for profit. Another holds the abridged preserve an earlier folk-pinghua archetype — a partial remnant of the pinghua layer in compressed form — with the hundred-chapter book expanding on top of them. The dispute is unresolved. Crucially, it is itself the best footnote to the pinghua layer's existence: if Shide tang were written from nothing, the abridged could only be its downstream cut; yet certain "same-yet-different" naïve narratives in the abridged (Tripitaka's origin, some subduing orders) hint they may descend from a more ancient oral-pinghua tradition. The pinghua layer likely never fully vanished but flowed, in compressed form, partially into the hundred-chapter book. The ancestor dispute matters precisely because it leads us back to that lost riverbed.

· · ·

Conclusion: A Stratified Riverbed ↔ cumulative canon

Stacking shihua, zaju, pinghua, and abridged layers, the Shide tang edition is no longer a "original scripture" born from nothing, but a riverbed pressed atop multiple accumulations: the shihua gave it the Monkey-Pilgrim seed; the zaju gave it the Great-Sage character and stage structure; the pinghua gave it the skeleton of "Wei Zheng beheads dragon" and the Chechi contest; the abridged may be the last channel by which that skeleton flowed into print. The so-called "original" was never a clear spring but a river constantly patched and overlaid. What we can touch is only the instant preserved by the carver's knife in 1592. The point of this supplement is not to fictionalize the river's full face, but to admit that "loss" itself is part of Journey's history — those lost pinghua, scattered manuscripts, and unnamed guild authors are equally the foundation on which the monkey took shape.
