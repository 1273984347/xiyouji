Key Insights
The 22 events divide almost evenly across the three lanes — 7 on Composition History, 7 on Version Evolution, 8 on Cultural Influence — which is itself the finding: the story of Journey to the West is not one line but three that keep re-synchronising. The lanes open far apart (composition starts in 629 with Xuanzang’s westward journey, version only in 1592 with the Shidetang hundred-chapter edition, influence in 1600 with the Ming opera adaptations) and then crowd together: 1955 is the only year where a composition and a version event land together (the Modern Collated Edition and the People’s Literature Collated Edition), and 6 of the 22 events fall between 1980 and 2020. Eleven of the 22 events — half the timeline — date from 1900 or later, 6 of them on the influence lane: by now the novel’s afterlife, not its composition, carries most of the timeline’s density.
