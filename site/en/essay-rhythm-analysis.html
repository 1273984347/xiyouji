The Music of the Verse — Four Dimensions of Sound-Law

ESSAY 35 · SUMMARY TRANSLATION
Four rhythm theorists (Wang Li, Qi Gong, Zhou Zhenfu, Zhu Guangqian) read four nodes (opening / furnace / spell / finale) through tone-level, parallelism, rhythm, and rounding-completion.
A summary translation of Journey to the West Poetry Rhythm Analysis (essay, Chinese). the Chinese original analyses the novel's prosody through four modern rhythm scholars and four sound-law dimensions (tone-level / parallelism / rhythm / rounding-completion), anchored at lines 522 / 864 / 1393 / 7085. All anchors are carried over without fabrication.

Sound-Law as the Bone of Verse ↔ ch. 1 / 7 / 14 / 100

Rhythm is the “bone of sound-law” in the novel's verse. Four modern scholars give four names to that bone — Wang Li names it tone-level (píngzè), Qi Gong names it sound-law (shēnglü), Zhou Zhenfu names it parallelism (duìzhàng), Zhu Guangqian names it rhythm (jiézòu). The novel's four anchor nodes map onto four dimensions: tone-level (ch. 1 line 522 opening verse), parallelism (ch. 7 line 864 the Eight-Trigram Furnace), rhythm (ch. 14 line 1393 the tightening spell), and rounding-completion (ch. 100 line 7085 the closing verse).

Tone-Level — the Opening Verse ↔ ch. 1, line 522

“Before chaos parted, heaven and earth were troubled…” runs the seven-character opening. Its tone pattern (“oblique-oblique-level-level-level-oblique-oblique”) follows Wang Li's interlace exactly, and it rhymes on oblique tones (“troubled / unseen / discerned”). Wu Cheng'en opens on an oblique rhyme to match the “troubled” chaos — short, hard oblique tones fit the unparted world. Tone is not mere rule here; it is the sound of heaven and earth opening.

Parallelism — the Eight-Trigram Furnace ↔ ch. 7, line 864

The furnace verse sets “fire” against “furnace,” “civil/martial” against “eight trigrams,” “forty-nine days” against “fiery eyes.” Qi Gong's sound-symmetry shows in the balanced pairs; his permissible variants show in the “1-3-5 free” licence that never breaks the “2-4-6” scaffold — the variant maps to “adjusting the fire,” the scaffold to “temper unchanging.”

Rhythm — the Tightening Spell ↔ ch. 14, line 1393

The spell's rhythm is urgent and tense: the Tang Monk chants quickly, Wukong's head aches with matching tension — Zhu Guangqian's “urgent rhythm matches tense feeling.” Zhou Zhenfu's flowing parallelism appears in “the mind-monkey submits / the six thieves vanish”: a running couplet where cause (submission) flows into effect (vanishing). Rhythm here is not pattern but the sound of constraint becoming release.

Rounding-Completion — the Closing Verse ↔ ch. 100, line 7085

The closing verse rounds the four dimensions into one: it closes on a level rhyme against the opening's oblique rhyme — an “oblique opening, level close” (zèqǐ píngshōu). Opening verse raises the rhyme, furnace carries it, spell turns it, finale answers it; four rhymes become one. The contemporary lesson: a poem needs the full path tone-level → parallelism → rhythm → completion — bone, joint, turn, and close.
