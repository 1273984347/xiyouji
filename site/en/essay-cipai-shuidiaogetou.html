Shuidiaogetou: The Long Lyric of Becoming

ESSAY · E19 · POETRY
A 95-character long lyric, set beside Su Shi — the meter as the mind-monkey's own Band-Tightening Spell.
A curated English summary of the project's Chinese essay Xīyóu yǔ Shuǐdiàogētóu Cípái Shǎngxī (A6 · W288). Theorists, dates, line anchors, and metric specs are taken from that source; no figures are invented. This is a summary translation, not a full rendering.

Introduction: The Long Lyric of Becoming ↔ a slow burn in ninety-five characters

The Shuidiaogetou (水调歌头, "Prelude to the Water Melody") is a classic changdiao — the long lyric. Its prosody: shuangdiao, ninety-five characters, nine lines in the upper counterpart (four level rhymes) and ten in the lower (four level rhymes). The project's Chinese专题 Xīyóu yǔ Shuǐdiàogētóu Cípái Shǎngxī (A6 · W288) reads the novel's four great nodes through a Shuidiaogetou lens as created realm → tempered nature → disciplined self → fulfilled realm (lines 522, 864, 1393, 7085) — and sets them beside Su Shi's famous lyric of 1076.

Four Realms Across the Four Nodes ↔ line anchors

  • Created realm — ch. 1, line 522 (stone monkey born). "Heaven's sincerity and the moon's essence... a stone egg, the wind makes a monkey." A realm born of asking — mirroring Su Shi's "when did the moon appear? I raise my cup to ask the sky."
  • Tempered nature — ch. 7, line 864 (the Bagua furnace). Forty-nine days of fire forge the Fiery Eyes — the "no-self" ordeal, and Ye Jiaying's weak virtue: the harsher theoutside, the firmer the inward character.
  • Disciplined self — ch. 14, line 1393 (the Band-Tightening spell). The spell "looks at things with the self," binding the mind-monkey — the "with-self realm," and weak virtue deepened: pain borne, yet the vow to protect the master kept.
  • Fulfilled realm — ch. 100, line 7085 (the five become perfected). From scenery (shallow) to affection (middle) to philosophy (deep) — Miao Yue's three layers — the long lyric closes on its final rhyme.

Su Shi's Lyric vs. the Four Nodes ↔ an ancient-antique counterpoint

"I want to ride the wind home, yet fear the jade towers' cold" (tempered)ch. 7, line 864 — Bagua furnaceBoth are "refined by ordeal" — Su's cold towers, Wukong's fiery eyes.
"Men have grief and joy, parting and meeting" (disciplined)ch. 14, line 1393 — the spellBoth are "virtue made by constraint" — parting's bond, the spell's bond.
"May we live long, sharing the moon's beauty" (fulfilled)ch. 100, line 7085 — apotheosisBoth close on a wish — long life's wish, the pilgrimage's fulfillment.

Conclusion: The Meter as a "Band-Tightening Spell" ↔ method

The Chinese专题's sharpest line: Shuidiaogetou's ninety-five-character statute is the lyric's own Band-Tightening Spell — the poet "forges" a good lyric inside the meter's constraint, just as Wukong "forges" his enlightenment inside the spell's. Constraint is not bondage but the rhythm of fulfillment. Su Shi compressed four realms into one short lyric; Wu Cheng'en unfolds them across eighty-one trials — yet both share the same "created → tempered → disciplined → fulfilled" structure. That structure is the method at the heart of the A6 cipai readings.
