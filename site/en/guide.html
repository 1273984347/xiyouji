READING GUIDE

You don't have to start at page one.

Annotated Journey to the West is a hybrid interpretation project: it does not read the original for you, but extends what comes after reading — from “what it is” to “why” and “what else”. It has four layers — the Source Text Layer (source/), the Interpretation Layer (characters / themes / cultural context), the Visualization Layer (this site's D3 interactive pages), and the Data Layer (reproducible text analysis). Below are reading paths by your role; every link points to a real, openable page on this site.

REFERENCE

Terms & conventions

ChapterThe unit of the original Journey to the West; 100 chapters in total.
Chapter titleThe title of each chapter, usually a couplet (e.g. “A Numinous Root Breeds the Source-Stream · Mind-Nature Cultivates the Great Way”).
Pilgrimage teamThe five: Tang Monk, Sun Wukong, Zhu Bajie, Sha Monk, White Dragon Horse.
81 ordealsThe 81 trials on the journey, symbolizing the structure of spiritual cultivation.
Havoc in HeavenChapters 1–7, Sun Wukong's revolt against the Heavenly court.
Western pilgrimageChapters 8–97, the main journey of Tang Monk and his disciples.
Return & enlightenmentChapters 98–100, return with the scriptures and attainment of the fruit.

On editions

This project's interpretation uses the Shide tang edition (Wanli 20th year, 1592 — the earliest complete extant woodblock) as the base text, collating other editions where necessary and noting discrepancies. Other important editions: the Qing Zhengdao edition (Daoist neidan commentary), the People's Literature Publishing House collated edition, and the Li Zhiwu critical edition (late-Ming pseudo-attributed commentary).

On citation

Cite the original by chapter title, format: “quote” — Chapter N. Cite others with source and link. For academic citation use GB/T 7714. This site is dual-licensed: code under MIT, text content under CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial); see LICENSE and LICENSE-CONTENT.md in the repository.
