VI · Key Insights

KEY INSIGHTS
  • Subnetwork structural differences: The Divine System shows a three-tier hierarchy (sovereignty–execution–dispatch); the Demon Faction shows four organizational forms (familial–sworn–brotherly–isolated); the Mortal Group shows a functional structure (witness–trial–gratitude). Persistence decreases across the three (Divine high → Demon mid → Mortal low).
  • Pilgrimage Team as broker: The Pilgrimage Team is the core node linking the three subnetworks. Cross-subnet edges account for 69.3%, showing the pilgrimage narrative is not a closed single network but an open multi-network interaction structure.
  • Divine rescue modes: The Divine System rescues the Pilgrimage Team in three modes — direct rescue (Guanyin subdues demons) / indirect rescue (Rulai handles ontic-level crises) / coordinating rescue (the Jade Emperor dispatches troops). The more organized the demons, the higher the divine rank required to rescue.
  • Demon organization & danger: Familial demons (the Bull Demon family) and sworn demons (the three Lion-Camel Ridge kings) are hardest to subdue, because they require facing multiple demons in coordination. Isolated demons are relatively easy but may have special abilities (e.g., the White Bone Demon’s shapeshifting).
  • Mortals’ functional & single-point nature: The Mortal Group has low co-occurrence (1–2 times) and plays functional rather than persistent roles. They appear at specific nodes, perform a specific function, then exit — contrasting with the divine’s persistence and the demons’ local persistence.
  • Ming-political mirror: The Divine System’s three-tier hierarchy mirrors Ming politics (imperial power–civil officials–dispatched posts); the Demon Faction’s familial form mirrors Ming vassal enfeoffment; the Mortal Group’s functionality mirrors modern stakeholders.
