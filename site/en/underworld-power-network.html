One · Underworld Power System Overview

UNDERWORLD SYSTEM OVERVIEW · DIYU BUREAUCRACY
Definition: In the world of Journey to the West, the Underworld is not a single judicial body but a composite system of five layers — "the Ten Yama Kings' bureaucracy + Judge Cui's execution + Kṣitigarbha's oversight + the mortal-realm bridge + Buddhist reception." The Ten Yama Kings — Qinguang, Chujiang, Songdi, Wuguan, Yanluo, Biancheng, Taishan, Dushi, Pingdeng, and Zhuanlun try souls by their sins in separate courts and form the bureaucratic core of the Underworld; Judge Cui (Cui Jue) — keeper of the Ledger of Life and Death, who at Wei Zheng's private request extends Emperor Taizong's lifespan; he is the most critical executive hub of the Underworld; Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva — the Buddhist realm's representative stationed in hell, who vowed "not to attain Buddhahood until hell is empty" and oversees rather than interferes in the trials; The Mortal-Realm Bridge — Wei Zheng (the dragon-slayer) and Liu Quan (the melon-bearer) pass between the yin and yang worlds in the flesh or in dreams, forming the interface between the Underworld and the living world; Buddhist Reception — Di Ting (Kṣitigarbha's mount) and the Welcoming Buddha (Lingyun Ferry) carry out the handoff between the Underworld and Lingshan. The core political-science paradox of the Underworld system: nominally independent of Heaven, it is in reality penetrated by both Heaven (Wei Zheng beheads the dragon in a dream at the Jade Emperor's command) and Lingshan (Kṣitigarbha's oversight).

Two · Underworld Power Force-Directed Graph

FORCE-DIRECTED GRAPH · NODE SIZE = POWER · COLOR = ROLE TYPE
Definition: Treats the 14 core Underworld figures as nodes; node size maps to "power strength (power 1–10)" and color maps to "role type". There are five edge types: Hierarchy (the ranking among the Ten Yama Kings), Subordination (Judge Cui dispatched by the Ten Yama Kings), Oversight (Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva's religious oversight of the Ten Yama Kings), Cross-realm (Wei Zheng's private channel to Heaven), and Affiliation (Liu Quan's bodily affiliation with the mortal realm). The force-directed graph directly reveals five topological forms: "the Ten Yama Kings clustered in hierarchy, Judge Cui executing from the center, Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva overseeing from above, Wei Zheng crossing to Heaven, and Liu Quan as the single point connecting to the mortal realm."

Three · Underworld–Heaven–Lingshan–Mortal Realm Sankey Diagram

SANKEY · UNDERWORLD ↔ HEAVEN ↔ LINGSHAN ↔ YANG-REALM
Definition: The Sankey diagram shows the power flows between the Underworld and the three realms. The Underworld system forms the left column (the Ten Yama Kings, Judge Cui, Kṣitigarbha, Wei Zheng, Liu Quan); the connected systems form the right column (Heaven, Lingshan, the mortal realm, and the Underworld itself). The flows reveal a political-science law of the Underworld system in Journey to the West: the Underworld is not an independent kingdom — the Ten Yama Kings are penetrated by both Heaven's establishment and Lingshan's oversight, and Judge Cui opens a back door to the mortal realm through private relationships, while Kṣitigarbha, in his Buddhist identity, sits in hell yet "will not attain Buddhahood until hell is empty" — the Underworld's "independence" is relative; in essence it is the buffer zone where Heaven, Lingshan, and the mortal realm contend.

Four · Underworld Character Ability Radar Chart

RADAR · 5 DIMENSIONS · JUDGMENT / EXECUTION / MAGIC / MERCY / INDEPENDENCE
Definition: Evaluates the comprehensive ability of Underworld figures across five dimensions — Judgment (the authority to pass sentence on souls' sins), Execution (the ability to carry out verdicts), Magic Power (supernatural transformations and martial arts), Compassion (pity toward the souls and the mortal realm), and Independence (the degree of freedom from Heaven's and Lingshan's interference). The larger the radar area, the greater the figure's overall Underworld power. Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva has the largest area but low independence (Buddhist oversight); the Ten Yama Kings have full Judgment but low Magic Power; Judge Cui has the lowest independence (subject to double requests); Wei Zheng has the highest independence (beheading the dragon in a dream as Heaven's envoy).

Five · Underworld Events Timeline

UNDERWORLD EVENTS TIMELINE · BY CHAPTER ORDER
Definition: Arranges the 7 key Underworld events in chapter order and displays them on separate tracks by the nature of each event. Each node marks a key event (erasing names / beheading the dragon / touring the Underworld / delivering melons / the Well Dragon King / Di Ting discerning the false / attaining perfection), and color distinguishes the stage: Conflict (a direct clash with the mortal realm), Communication (negotiation between the yin and yang worlds), and Resolution (final closure). The timeline presents a three-part rhythm: "dense early (the Chapter 3–11 Emperor Taizong Underworld arc), sparse in the middle (only the Well Dragon King and Di Ting), and a closing convergence (Chapter 100, when the Five Saints attain perfection and cross between yin and yang)."

Event Stage Distribution (donut chart)

  • The Underworld's independence is relative: Although Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva vowed "not to attain Buddhahood until hell is empty," he is in essence Lingshan's religious oversight of the Underworld; although Judge Cui holds the Ledger of Life and Death, he privately alters Emperor Taizong's lifespan at Wei Zheng's request — the Underworld judges nominally independently, yet is in reality penetrated by both Heaven's establishment (Wei Zheng beheads the dragon in a dream at the Jade Emperor's command) and Lingshan's oversight (Kṣitigarbha stationed there).
  • Judge Cui is the political hub of the Underworld: Among the 14 Underworld figures, Judge Cui simultaneously connects three parties — the Ten Yama Kings (subordination), the mortal realm (Wei Zheng's request), and the emperor (the private lifespan alteration) — making him the mediating node of the Underworld's power network. His presence lets "private requests" pierce the "bureaucratic trial" — the largest institutional loophole in the Underworld system of Journey to the West.
  • Underworld events cluster at the beginning and the end: Of the 7 key events, 5 are concentrated in Chapters 3–11 (the Emperor Taizong Underworld arc + Wukong erasing names + Liu Quan delivering melons), while 2 are spread across Chapter 58 and Chapter 100. This distribution reveals that in the pilgrimage narrative the Underworld is a "starting device" (establishing the rules of the Ledger of Life and Death) and an "ending device" (the Five Saints attaining perfection and crossing between yin and yang), while it is almost absent in the middle — its dramatic function is structural rather than procedural.
