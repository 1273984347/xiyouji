③ Four-Dimension Cross Insights

[Coupling] Rescue Count and Difficulty Level are tightly coupled. Among the 8 extreme hardships, 7 have a rescue count ≥ 2, while 21 of the 24 easy hardships have a rescue count of 0. "Calling for backup" is the most direct proxy for difficulty — whether a trial can be resolved independently sets the ceiling on its difficulty.
[Polarization] Battle Duration is polarized. Short (resolved in one chapter) accounts for 41 hardships (50.6%), Long (4+ chapters) for 18 (22.2%), with only 22 in the middle. This echoes the "opening-development-turn-conclusion" structure of Ming chapter novels — key plotlines unfold over many chapters, while transitional episodes pass in a single chapter.
[Treasure] Borrowed divine treasures cluster in the extreme tier. Of the 9 borrows, 6 occur in the 9-10 extreme tier — Princess Iron Fan's banana-leaf fan requires Lingji Bodhisattva's Wind-Fixing Pill, and the Green Ox Demon requires the Tathāgata's hint about the Diamond Bracelet's origin. Borrowing a treasure is essentially "the gods and Buddhas intervening indirectly."
[Exception] Thrice-Beating the White-Bone Demon (Ch. 27) is a counterexample of "low rescue, high difficulty". Difficulty 6 but rescue count 0 — the White-Bone Demon has no backing and Wukong resolves it alone, yet the "master-disciple infighting" inner-demon dimension is extremely high, forming a classic "easy to defeat, hard to pass" inner-demon trial.
[Peak] The three Lion-Camel Ridge demons (Ch. 74) top all four dimensions. Difficulty 10 / Long duration / 3+ rescues / borrowed treasure — this is the novel's only "all-four-peak" trial. The Buddha personally leads the five hundred Arhats to subdue the Roc, marking the absolute limit of celestial mobilization against a pilgrimage trial.
