① 15-Demon Capability Radar Overlay

Rendered with D3.js radarChart. The 5-dimensional capabilities of 15 demons (Magic Power / Strategy / Artifact / Background / Ending) are overlaid as translucent polygons; click a demon name to toggle highlighting. The overlay reveals two archetypal demon types: "high-strategy, no-background" (White Bone Demon / Six-Eared Macaque) and "high-background, all-dimension" (Golden-Winged Roc / Yellow Brow King).

② Five-Dimension Capability Ranking Bar Chart

Statistics for the highest-scoring demon and the average across the five capability dimensions. "Magic Power" has the highest ceiling (10 points · Golden-Winged Roc), while "Ending" has the lowest average (4.7 points) — of the 15 demons, 6 have an ending score of ≤2, so the demon group's overall "good-ending rate" is under 40%.

③ Magic Power vs. Ending Scatter Plot

The horizontal axis is Magic Power (1-10) and the vertical axis is Ending (1-10). The distribution of the 15 demons reveals an inverse trend: "the higher the magic power, the worse the ending" — Six-Eared Macaque (Magic Power 9 / Ending 1) and Golden-Winged Roc (Magic Power 10 / Ending 6) form a stark contrast. The red dashed line is the average-ending reference line.

④ Demon Classification Sankey Diagram (Origin → Ending)

The left side has four origin categories (Heaven / Buddhist Realm / Mortal Realm / Wild) and the right side has four ending categories (Recruited / Killed / Escaped / Recalled). The flow reveals that "demons from Heaven and the Buddhist Realm were mostly recruited (9)", while "wild and mortal-realm demons were mostly killed (7)" — endings are tightly coupled with origins, and background determines fate.

⑤ Capability Extremes KPI Cards

Four extreme demons: Strongest Magic Power (Golden-Winged Roc · the Tathāgata's own uncle), Most Strategic (Six-Eared Macaque · an indistinguishable fake Wukong), Most Tragic (Six-Eared Macaque · dies as soon as the Tathāgata sees through him), and Luckiest (Red Boy · recruited as the Boy of Wealth). Strategy and Ending form a stark contrast.

[Contrast] Six-Eared Macaque: "second in strategy, worst in ending". A 9/10 Strategy paired with a 1/10 Ending — he could impersonate Wukong and deceive the entire pilgrimage team and the gods of Heaven, yet he could not fool the Tathāgata Buddha. "Strategy" fails before absolute power, the sharpest irony in the whole book.
[Recruited] The "luckiest" Red Boy was no weakling. With Magic Power 8 / Strategy 7, Red Boy was taken in by Guanyin as the Boy of Wealth — appearing "subdued" while actually "attaining enlightenment". This contrasts with White Bone Demon (Magic Power 4 / Ending 2) — the prerequisite for being recruited is "being useful".
