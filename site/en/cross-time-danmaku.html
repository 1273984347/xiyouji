Key Insights

What five centuries of commentary actually show

  • 01The star map holds 10 critics across 5 dynasties, and every one of them argues from a different seat — Li Zhuowu (Ming interpreter), Jin Shengtan (Qing commentator), Lu Xun, Hu Shi, Chen Yinke, Zheng Zhenduo, Mao Zedong, Lin Yutang, Qian Zhongshu and Guo Moruo (modern) — from "gods and demons all have human feeling" to "at most it is a very entertaining comic novel".
  • 02The same quotes are re-cut for four age tiers — the 4-tier wall (Preschool / Elementary · Middle School · High School · University) turns Lu Xun's line into an emoji wall for the youngest tier and into a structured debate topic for the middle-school tier.
  • 03Only 3 critics reached the world map, and each through a different channel — Pearl S. Buck (United States, The English translation Monkey), Jorge Luis Borges (Argentina) and Kenzaburō Ōe (Japan): the English-language translation is the vector that carried the book abroad.
  • 04The 3 recorded global adaptations split by medium, not by theme — Japan took the anime route (Dragon Ball), South Korea the comic route and the United States the film route (The Forbidden Kingdom); the 4 modern-commentary entries meanwhile hang the book on Big Tech, AI anxiety, social-media personas and involution.
