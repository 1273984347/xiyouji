INTRODUCTION · Xiyouji as a Cultivation Allegory

Journey to the West is not merely a divine-monster adventure novel, but a complete cultivation allegory. This page dissects its philosophical essence along two dimensions: the Mind-Monkey Nature Curve (Wukong's 6-stage mind-nature evolution) and the Cultivation Allegory Map (the 81 hardships mapped to 8 Buddhist mind-nature trials). Core proposition: the so-called scripture quest is really the process of taming the mind-monkey and thought-horse.

Core thesis: the true protagonist of Journey to the West is not Tang Sanzang but Wukong's "mind". The so-called "mind-monkey" is the restless, agitated mind of Buddhist terminology — Wukong's 6-stage mind-nature evolution (indulgence → suppression → awakening → enlightenment) fully presents an ordinary person's mind-cultivation journey from indulgence to enlightenment. The 81 hardships are not external calamities but the externalization of inner obstacles — each monster corresponds to one inner obstacle (greed / anger / ignorance / pride / doubt / lust / fame / death), and each demon-subduing is an act of breaking a mental attachment.

Dual perspective: this page presents this cultivation allegory through two complementary dimensions: the micro perspective — Wukong's individual 5-dimension mind-nature evolution (anger / pride / greed / wisdom / restraint); the macro perspective — the overall mapping of the 81 hardships to 8 kinds of mind-nature trials. The two are linked by the Heart Sutra of Chapter 19 as the intellectual through-line — the Heart Sutra teaches "emptiness", the 81 hardships teach "breaking attachment", and "form is not different from emptiness" means breaking attachment and returning to truth.

Reading the four stages: Indulgence (birth + Havoc in Heaven) — the natural true mind, through indulgence, reaches an anger peak (anger 9) and must be suppressed under Five Elements Mountain; Suppression (early pilgrimage · submission) — tamed by the Tight-Fillet Spell, the mind-monkey is constrained by external force but anger still erupts (anger 7); Awakening (mid-pilgrimage · balance) — the Six-Eared Macaque incident subdues the two minds, and wisdom begins to grow (wisdom 7); Enlightenment (awakening stage + buddhahood) — the mind-monkey unifies, turning from external constraint to inner self-awareness, until the tight fillet falls off by itself and buddhahood is reached.

Not simple linearity, but a spiral ascent: the core feature of the mind-nature curve is spiral ascent, not linear decline — at Havoc in Heaven wisdom_level actually drops to 4 (indulgence clouds wisdom); in early pilgrimage restraint_level is only 4 (external force, not internalized); only after the Six-Eared Macaque incident do wisdom and restraint leap together (7→9, 7→9) — this is the inner transformation after "subduing the two minds", not the continuation of external constraint. At final buddhahood, anger=0, wisdom=10, restraint=10, reaching the ultimate balance of mind-nature.

Key turning point · the Six-Eared Macaque incident (Ch. 58): the true-vs-false Monkey King = subduing the two minds, the mind-nature turning point of the whole book. Before this, Wukong's practice is passive (tamed by the external Tight-Fillet Spell, restraint only 1→4); after this, Wukong begins to actively internalize executive control (restraint leaps 4→7→9→10). This turn reveals the essence of mind-cultivation — true awakening begins with "subduing the two minds", that is, no more inner contradiction and division; the mind-monkey unifies, turning from external constraint to inner self-awareness. This is the beginning of "thoroughly subduing the mind-monkey", not its endpoint.
