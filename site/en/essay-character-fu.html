Portrait Poems — Fu as Character-Making

ESSAY 34 · SUMMARY TRANSLATION
About 60 portrait-fu among 800+ poems; four fu-types (debut / transformation / enlightenment / apotheosis) read through Liu Xie, Zhong Rong, Sikong Tu, and Wang Guowei.
A summary translation of Character-Portrait Fu Poetry Appreciation (essay, Chinese). the Chinese original reads the novel's character-portrait verse through four classical fu-theorists and four portrait types (debut / transformation / enlightenment / apotheosis), with chapter-line anchors 522 / 864 / 1393 / 7085. All anchors are carried over without fabrication.

A Portrait Tradition Inside the Verse ↔ ch. 1 / 7 / 14 / 100

Of the novel's 800-plus poems, roughly 60 are portrait-fu (“character fu,” rénfù) — set-pieces that fix a figure's appearance, bearing, and destiny in verse. They do four jobs: debut portraiture (fixing the image at first entrance), battle narrative (describing both combatants mid-fight), enlightenment (a deity's verse that awakens a mortal), and closing appraisal (judging a figure at apotheosis). Four anchor lines carry the argument: ch. 1 line 522 (the stone monkey's birth — debut portrait), ch. 7 line 864 (Havoc in Heaven — transformation portrait), ch. 14 line 1393 (the tightening circlet — enlightenment portrait), ch. 100 line 7085 (the Five Sages perfected — apotheosis portrait).

Debut Portrait — the Stone Monkey ↔ ch. 1, line 522

“Complete in five senses, whole in four limbs… from his eyes stream two shafts of golden light, piercing the Palace of Poles.” This is the debut portrait: the first clause embodies the object (senses, limbs, golden light), the second expresses the intent (a rebellious nature already implied). The golden gaze threads the whole arc — from the Great Sage Equal-to-Heaven to the Victorious Buddha.

Transformation Portrait — Havoc in Heaven ↔ ch. 7, line 864

The duel with Erlang: Wukong becomes a sparrow, Erlang a hawkeagle; Wukong a fish, Erlang a kingfisher; Wukong a bird, Erlang a bowman. The transformation portrait is Liu Xie's “display” at full stretch — a catalogue of mutual shape-shifting that is itself the fight.

Enlightenment Portrait — the Circlet ↔ ch. 14, line 1393

Guanyin's Heart-Sutra verse — “form is not other than emptiness, emptiness not other than form” — is the enlightenment portrait. A few words imply infinite “mind-nature” resonance: Sikong Tu's “meaning beyond the words” made verse.

Apotheosis Portrait — the Five Sages ↔ ch. 100, line 7085

Tang = Candana-Merit Buddha, Wukong = Victorious Buddha, Bajie = Altar-Cleaner, Sha = Golden-Body Arhat, Ao Lie = Eightfold Dragon-horse. The apotheosis portrait is both made (a fictional Buddhist apotheosis) and written (a real record of cultivation's end) — Wang Guowei's two worlds resolved.

A Ming Mirror ↔ archaism · xinling · song-speech

The portrait-fu is a literary echo of three Ming currents: the Archaist movement (Li Mengyang, Wang Shizhen — “prose must be Qin-Han, poetry must be High Tang”) in its return to Han-fu grandeur; the Gong'an school's xinling (“utter one's true sensibility, brook no fixed form”) in Wukong's and Bajie's undisguised natures; and the chàngbái (song-speech) of Kunqu and Yiyang opera, which lets the fu be both chanted and sung.
